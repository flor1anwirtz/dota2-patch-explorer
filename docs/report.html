A large-scale observational analysis of ranked matches

Abstract

This study compares Radiant and Dire win rates in two patch-labelled Dota 2 ranked-match samples. After filtering, 938,598 matches in patch 7.41 and 500,000 in patch 7.42 were retained. Radiant win rates were 53.01% and 50.56%, respectively. The observed side-to-side gap narrowed from 6.03 to 1.11 percentage points. Both samples depart from equal win rates under the original binomial test assumptions. Duration and rank stratification provide descriptive context; unequal sampling periods and possible dependence between matches limit causal and population-level conclusions.

Data and methods

The original analysis uses OpenDota public matches and restricts the sample to ranked lobbies, at least 20 minutes in duration, recognized match IDs and outcomes, and selected average-rank tier codes. The file names provide the patch labels. Exact duplicate rows are removed, and conflicting match IDs or cross-sample overlap trigger an error. See data documentation for the complete filter and variable definitions.

Within each patch, a two-sided exact binomial test evaluates the null hypothesis of a 50% Radiant win rate. A two-proportion test without continuity correction compares the patch samples. Very small p-values are calculated on the log scale. Exact binomial confidence intervals are computed for Radiant shares and complemented for Dire. Match duration is grouped into [20,30), [30,40), and 40+ minutes; rank is grouped as Herald–Crusader, Archon–Ancient, and Divine/Immortal.

The original two-proportion comparison gave p = 7.1206e-174. As a supplementary effect-size calculation from the archived win counts, the change in Radiant share (7.42 minus 7.41) is -2.459 percentage points, with an approximate independent-binomial 95% interval of [-2.630, -2.287] percentage points. This interval is not adjusted for clustering or selection.

Mean durations were 41.17 and 41.96 minutes; median durations were 40.10 and 40.68 minutes. Both rank distributions are concentrated in Archon and Legend, although they are not identical.

The earlier-duration groups exhibit larger Radiant advantages. In the 40+ minute stratum, Radiant wins fewer than half of sampled matches in both patches. The three rank groups exhibit a smaller Radiant advantage in 7.42 than in 7.41.

Discussion and limitations

The observed gap is substantially narrower in the later sample, despite a statistically detectable departure from equal win rates. Effect sizes are therefore more informative than p-values alone. The samples cover unequal calendar windows and were not randomly assigned to versions. Player composition, match availability, time of day and other changes could explain part of the difference. Rank and duration summaries do not rule out confounding. In particular, duration is an outcome of gameplay, so conditioning on it does not identify a causal patch effect. Repeated players and temporal dependence could invalidate the independent-match inference assumptions. The filter excludes matches shorter than 20 minutes and rank codes outside the original list, which limits generalization. Patch labels have not been independently verified from a patch variable.

Reproducibility and next steps

This report uses archived outputs from the original R notebook. It is an observational research report, not a peer-reviewed publication. The raw CSVs are required for a full rerun. Future work could verify patch membership, improve sampling across matched calendar windows, examine dependence-aware uncertainty, and assess sensitivity to duration and rank filters. A regression analysis with patch-by-rank interactions is a possible extension, not a completed result.

Data source: OpenDota. Original notebook and refactored code are included in the repository.

Eligibility rules

Ranked lobby, duration at least 1,200 seconds, recognized win indicator, positive numeric match ID string, rank code 11–15, 21–25, …, 71–75, or exactly 80. Divine and Immortal are pooled. The original rank rule does not include every code above 80. Exact duplicate rows are removed, and conflicting IDs or cross-patch overlap stop the pipeline. The descriptive mean medal averages ordinal medal steps with Immortal mapped to Divine 5.
